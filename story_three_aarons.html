The Three Aarons

Proving the father of Freeman Jones Searcy — and correcting, against a hostile primary, a link this project itself once carried.

Status of this finding: the project's earlier conclusion has been Corrected. What is now documented is a disproof — the father of Freeman Jones Searcy was not the son of Captain William Hargrove Searcy. Who his father was remains Open. This page has been rewritten to reflect the correction; an earlier version of it reached the opposite conclusion.

The problem: which Aaron?

For a generation the Searcy patriline stalled on a single, deceptively simple question: who was the father of Freeman Jones Searcy (1830–1908, ancestor #16), the great-great-grandfather through whom the Searcy surname descends to DeWayne? Everyone agreed the answer was "an Aaron Searcy." The trouble was that the early-nineteenth-century Searcy diaspora holds more than one man of that name — and the competing accounts each attached Freeman to a different Aaron. Choose the wrong one, and every generation above him is wrong too.

Freeman himself is firmly documented from 1830 forward: born in Bibb County, Alabama; married to Mary Jane Rorie in Alcorn County, Mississippi; enumerated across the northeast-Mississippi censuses to his death in 1908. But "Aaron Searcy" is not a unique key, and the derivative genealogies had quietly conflated distinct men. Three accounts were in circulation, and they could not all be true.

The three accounts

Account 1 — the workbook's Aaron (William's son)

The project's own earlier reconstruction placed Freeman's father as Aaron Searcy, son of Captain William Hargrove Searcy, the Revolutionary War officer of Guilford County, North Carolina. Its strongest evidence was a birth register in William's own hand, preserved in his widow's pension file, naming a son "Aaron Searcy — born May the 7 — 1790." The chain was plausible and the instrument was primary — but it belonged to a different Aaron.

Account 2 — W. Terry Searcy's "different Aaron"

The published Searcy researcher W. Terry Searcy, in his study The Searcy Family of Early America — the Tale of Three Aarons, challenged that identification. He argued that casual tree-building had merged distinct Aarons, and that Freeman's father was a separate man — born about 1797, the "Tishomingo Man" — not Captain William's son at all. Long treated by this project as the challenger's view, it is the account the primary record now confirms.

Account 3 — the "descend from Peter" story

A third strand, carried in a FamilySearch community tree and a Searcy newsletter, held that the Tishomingo Searcys descended through Peter Searcy. Peter was in fact Captain William's eldest son, and he really did migrate to Tishomingo County and die there — which is exactly why the confusion took hold. But Peter was not Freeman's father either.

Three accounts; one father. They could not be settled by weighing the secondary trees against one another — those trees were the source of the confusion, not its cure. It took primary documents that spoke directly to this Aaron. Two of them exist, and read together they overturn the account the project had adopted.

Why the register misled us

Pension file R.9342 is genuine, and its birth register — written on the back of the title page of a 1768 Glasgow edition of The Pilgrim's Progress and certified in 1845 as being "in the handwriting of the said William Searcy" — really does record a son, "Aaron Searcy — born May the 7 — 1790." The register is not in doubt. The mistake was assuming that this Aaron, William's son, was the same man as the Aaron who fathered Freeman in Alabama and died in Tishomingo County, Mississippi. Two men, one name; the register documents the wrong one.

The register even supplies the reason the error was so tempting. William and Sarah's registered children, in birth order, were Mary (1780), Peter (1782), Elizabeth (1784), Cary (1787), Aaron (1790), and Sarah (1793). A son named Aaron; a son named Peter who moved to Tishomingo — the very county Freeman's family settled. Every hook the confusion needed sat inside that one family. What the register does not contain is the detail that breaks the case open.

The decisive instrument: a Mississippi courtroom

Our Aaron — the Tishomingo Man — left a document of his own, and it is a hostile one: a lawsuit. He had bought a tract of Tishomingo land, put the title in his sister's name, given a note for the purchase, and defaulted. The note was sold, the land went to public sale, and the clouded title carried the dispute all the way to the Mississippi High Court of Errors and Appeals, where it was reported as Walker v. Williams (30 Miss. *166). In laying out the facts, the court names Aaron's sister.

The land, the court recounted, had been bought for "Susan Bow, his sister" — who had married E. F. Boren of Henderson County, Tennessee.

Susan is the knockout. Captain William Hargrove Searcy's registered daughters were Mary, Elizabeth, and Sarah — there is no Susan among his children. An Aaron whose sister is Susan cannot be the Aaron in William's register, whose sisters were Mary, Elizabeth, and Sarah. The two Aarons are two men. And the sister is not a lone thread: Susan Searcy Bow Boren appears with her husband Elijah F. Boren in the 1850 census of Henderson County, Tennessee, and our Aaron has a probable brother as well — Irwin Searcy (b. 1809, Georgia), who followed him into Mississippi. None of these people are in William's family. The link the project had carried as documented is disproved.

Disambiguating the three Aarons

With the register set aside for our line, W. Terry Searcy's framework is the one that holds. Freeman's father is the Tishomingo Man, born about 1797 — the age the 1850 Tishomingo census gives him (53) back-calculates to, exactly as Terry read it. The corrected chain runs:

[parents not yet proven] → Aaron Searcy (b. ca. 1797, the "Tishomingo Man") → Freeman Jones Searcy (b. 1830) → Robert Edwin Searcy → Lester → Daniel → DeWayne

Terry's three Aarons — set out in his own study and now corroborated by the court record — are genuinely distinct men: Aaron the Elder (b. ca. 1763, of the Georgia head-right lands, probably a son of Daniel Searcy of Johnston County, N.C.); Aaron the Middle Man (b. ca. 1790, son of George Searcy of Cumberland County, N.C., who lived and died in Baldwin County, Georgia); and Aaron the Younger, the "Tishomingo Man" (b. ca. 1797), our ancestor. To these three the pension register adds a fourth Aaron of the wider surname — Captain William's son, born 1790 — the man the project had mistaken for ours. Naming them apart is the whole resolution: the confusion arose precisely because these men had been collapsed into one.

Peter, too, is accounted for. The "descend from Peter" story confused Captain William's son Peter — who really did settle in Tishomingo — with our family. He was neither Freeman's father nor our Aaron's brother; he simply shared the county.

What the correction costs, and what it leaves standing

An honest finding states its own price. The cost is real: cutting Aaron loose from Captain William Hargrove Searcy detaches the entire Searcy patriline the project had built above him — William (b. 1754, a correct date for a man who is simply no longer ours), his father William the elder, and John Searcy Sr. of Granville County — and with it the Searcy family's Revolutionary War / DAR path, which ran through Captain William's service at Guilford Court House. That path can no longer be claimed on this line. The Searcy surname line now stops, honestly, at Aaron of Tishomingo, with his parents unproven.

What is not affected: the deep lines that reach back to the Swiss de Graffenrieds and the English Needhams, and the Revolutionary Patriots among the Johnson–DeGraffenreid ancestors, all descend to DeWayne through his maternal branches, not through the Searcy patriline. They are untouched by this correction. The tree loses six people at the top of a single line; it does not lose its depth.

And the open question is now stated plainly: who were the parents of Aaron the Tishomingo Man? Terry's reading — that he is likely a grandson of the Johnston/Cumberland County, N.C., Searcys, perhaps through Aaron the Elder — is the leading hypothesis, with brother Irwin and sister Susan as the FAN-cluster handles to test it. That work is open, and it is named here so that no reader mistakes a corrected line for a completed one.

Credit where it is owed

This correction is W. Terry Searcy's before it is ours. He identified the several Aarons and distinguished them in print in The Searcy Family of Early America — the Tale of Three Aarons; he placed Freeman's father as the b. ca. 1797 Tishomingo Man; and he pointed to the Mississippi appellate case that names the sister. What this project added was verification against the primary itself — reading Walker v. Williams in the reports, confirming Susan (Bow) Boren in the 1850 Henderson County census, and fixing the marriage of Aaron and Martha "Patsy" Jones to Bibb County, Alabama, Marriage Book 5, p. 6, on 21 September 1819. The project's earlier page reached the opposite conclusion and dismissed Terry's date as a census artifact. It was wrong, and Terry was right. Testing a cherished link against a hostile document and losing the link is not a failure of the method — it is the method.

Key sources

  • Walker v. Williams, 30 Miss. *166 (Mississippi High Court of Errors and Appeals) — the decisive primary: recounts that Aaron Searcy bought the land for "Susan Bow, his sister," who had intermarried with E. F. Boren of Henderson County, Tennessee. Located and supplied as a lead by W. Terry Searcy.
  • W. Terry Searcy, The Searcy Family of Early America — the Tale of Three Aarons — the study that distinguished the Aarons and identified Freeman's father as the b. ca. 1797 "Tishomingo Man."
  • 1850 U.S. Census, Tishomingo County, Mississippi, household 760 — "Aaron Searcy," age 53 (→ b. ca. 1797), the reading Terry drew and the census this finding upholds.
  • 1850 U.S. Census, Henderson County, Tennessee — Susan Searcy Bow Boren with husband Elijah F. Boren, corroborating the court-named sister.
  • Bibb County, Alabama, Marriage Book 5, p. 6 — Aaron Searcy × Martha "Patsy" F. Jones, 21 September 1819 (Terry's study gives 1817; the marriage-book entry fixes the exact date).
  • Revolutionary War pension file R.9342 (Capt. William Hargrove Searcy / widow Sarah), NARA M804, roll 2144 — the birth register naming William's son "Aaron Searcy — born May the 7 — 1790," the man formerly, and mistakenly, identified as Freeman's father. Retained here to document why the two Aarons were confused, and how the register was misapplied.
