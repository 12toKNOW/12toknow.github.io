A Cast of Georges

The Wymondley line runs thick with George Needhams — and the book, the county history and the workbook each name them differently. Here is which is which, and which two are yours.

Gentry families reused given names, and the Wymondley Needhams reused George hardest of all. But the real trouble is not that there were several Georges. It is that three separate naming schemes are running over the same men at once: the book calls them George I, George II and George III; the county history does the same; the workbook calls them George Sr. and George Jr. and gives Ahnentafel numbers. Line the schemes up and the confusion mostly dissolves. Only two of these men carry your descent.

James the Surveyor › John of Wymondley › George I · #18320 › Eustace › George Sr. · #4580 › Maurice › Barbara

Corrected 2 September 2026. Two things changed on this page. First, an earlier version treated “George I” as a narrative label for a died-out senior line — it is not; George I is a documented man, the one in the first card below. Second, the 1634 Visitation was re-read from the page image rather than a transcription, which showed that Eustace was George I's son, not his grandson. John Needham of Welwyn, once carried as the generation between them, is Eustace's younger brother. The direct line lost a generation, so every Ahnentafel number above Eustace moved down one doubling — the George who was #36640 is now #18320.

George Needham of Wymondley — “George I” #18320 · direct

b. ca. 1556 (16 in 1572) · m. Margaret Stile, 25 Sep 1581, Beckenham, Kent · d. 27 Jun 1626, bur. Little Wymondley chancel
Direct ancestor · 12×-great-grandfather
WhoThe 1581 Beckenham groom — “George Nedam, son and heir, is 16 yeres old” in the 1572 Visitation his father John of Wymondley signed. He held the Priory from his own father's death in 1591/92 until his death at seventy. He erected the 1605 monument in the chancel to his father and grandfather — the brass whose inscription is the reason we know James was the King's Surveyor. He was lay impropriator of the Wymondley Parva rectory from at least 1614. In 1615 he settled the manor on his son Eustace at Eustace's marriage.
IsThe man the book calls George I of Wymondley and VCH calls “George Needham the first.” Same person, two labels. He is Eustace's father.
NotNot George Sr. below — that is his grandson, sixty years later. Not his own second son John of Welwyn, who took the cadet seat and whose line runs off to one side.

George Needham Sr. — “George II” #4580 · direct

bp. 16 Aug 1618, Little Wymondley · will 4 Mar 1666/67, proved 10 Aug 1679 · d. 1669
Direct ancestor · 10×-great-grandfather
WhoEustace's son — “George Needham, son and heir, 16 years old, 1634.” Held The Priory. Lord of the manors of Little Wymondley and Graveley Hall. Father of Maurice (#2290), so Barbara Tempest Needham's grandfather. His will names four sons and three daughters; his tax and household notes survive on the fly-leaves of his great-great-grandfather's pay-books in the Bodleian.
NotNot his grandfather George I. And not his own eldest son George Jr. — your line runs through the younger son Maurice, not the heir.

George Needham Jr. — “George III” collateral · no Ahn. #

b. ca. 1645 · eldest son and heir of George Sr. · active 1666–1684 · m. Lidia
Collateral — your 10×-great-granduncle
WhoGeorge Sr.'s eldest son and co-executor of the 1666/67 will (with his mother Barbara); the “George Nedham” in the 1671–78 Nedham v. Nedham family-property suits; with his wife Lidia, the father of a Barbara Nedham baptized 1673 at Little Wymondley.
NotNot your ancestor — your line runs through his younger brother Maurice. And his daughter Barbara (1673) is not your Barbara Tempest (bp. 1685): two Barbaras, a cousinship and twelve years apart.

The last George of the Priory collateral · line ends here

d. 1725, without male issue · daughters Barbara (m. John Sherwin) and Martha (m. Thomas Browne)
Collateral — where the Wymondley seat runs out
WhoThe George who held the Priory after George Sr. and died in 1725 leaving no son. His two daughters were the heirs; Martha married Thomas Browne, the land surveyor who drew the 1731 estate map of the Priory. The manor was sold to Samuel Vanderplank in 1733 — ending nearly two hundred years of Needham tenancy that had begun with the 1537 grant to James.
OpenWhether this is George Jr. himself (VCH counts only three Georges after 1626) or George Jr.'s son born 1672 (Vol. I ch. 4's “cousin George,” who would make a fourth) is not yet settled. Either way he is off your line — Barbara had already sailed for Charleston in 1713.

Rev. George Needham of London collateral · ruled out of the line

b. ca. 1582 · Christ Church, Oxford 1600 · Vicar of Horley 1613, rector of South Heighton 1617
Once mistaken for your ancestor — retracted
WhoA younger son of George I — and therefore, on the corrected reading, Eustace's own brother. He took holy orders. He is the “George of Oxford” of the early research, and the brother and executor named in the 1611/1613 will of Arthur Nedham of London.
NotAn earlier pass (the CT-017 memo) proposed him as Eustace's father. Retracted. He is a collateral clergyman, off the line — but a closer one than we thought: brother, not uncle.
The one line to keep in your ear: only two Georges carry your blood — George I (#18320), Eustace's father and the man who set the 1605 brass, and George Sr. (#4580), Barbara's grandfather. Every other George here — the heir George Jr., the last George of the Priory, the clergyman of London — is a side branch. And when the tape says the Priory came to Eustace, it came to him from his own father, in a straight line, at his marriage in 1615.

Where this comes from

The direct chain rests on the 1572 Visitation of Hertfordshire (p. 16, signed “By me JOHN NEDHAM”) and the 1634 Visitation (pp. 77–78), printed in Harleian Society vol. XXII (Metcalfe, 1886), together with the Ancestral Workbook v7.15 and the §3 Wymondley Supplement. The 1634 pages were re-read from the page image on 2 September 2026: the “A” continuation marker beneath George and Margaret Stile at the foot of p. 77 heads a single wide bar on p. 78 whose left end drops to “John Needham of Wellwyn, 2 son” and whose right end runs down the margin into “Eustace Needham of Little Wymondley, Esq., eldest son.” They are brothers.

The regnal numbering and the manorial descent — George the first d. 1626, George the younger d. 1669, his successor d. without male issue 1725, the 1615 and 1623 settlements, the 1733 sale — are from the Victoria County History, A History of the County of Hertford, vol. 3 (1912), 185–191, “Parishes: Little Wymondley.” George I's death date and the 1605 monument are from the Wymondley Parva register and Sir Henry Chauncy, The Historical Antiquities of Hertfordshire (1700), p. 362.

George Jr. is documented in George Sr.'s will (TNA PROB 11/330/451) and the 1671–78 Chancery suits catalogued in the CT-017 memo; Rev. George of London is in the workbook's Sibling Collaterals (Alumni Oxonienses; CT-017 “George of Oxford” retraction). The narrative labels George I / II / III are used in I Am Coming, Vol. I and follow VCH.
