The Professional Foundation: Know Your Red Line
"There is a near 100 percent probability that YOU will be faced with an ethical dilemma."

Two non-negotiable truths I told my students:

1. Know your red line. What are you not willing to do from an ethical perspective? Define it NOW, not in the heat of the moment. "The reputation of a thousand years may be determined by the conduct of one hour." (Japanese proverb)

2. You WILL be asked to cross it. And here's what happens if you do: They have you. They know you went beyond your red line, and they will ask you to go further. And further. And further.

Or, you can say NO. Be willing to suffer the consequences. Get fired if necessary.

"It often requires more courage to dare to do right than to fear to do wrong." — Abraham Lincoln

Here's why you CAN say no: You can always get another job, especially if you keep working to be invaluable and better skilled than your peers. This is why "Never be outworked" matters. Your skill set is your freedom to maintain your ethics.

You cannot waver on your red line. Everything else is negotiable. Your ethics are not.

💭 Wisdom from the Journey

Real lessons learned over decades of zigzagging

🎯 Quantify First, Then Qualify
"Let the data do the talking, first. Then let's discuss how current factors provide grounds to alter the path forward as told by the data." Start somewhere—even Excel can be your friend. There's real money to be saved (and made) through data analysis.
🐢 Slow Down to Speed Up
"I can evaluate situations and make quick decisions. But I've discovered there's an important part missing: slowing my brain." Take time to "Puddy out" (yes, Seinfeld reference). By thinking about nothing for a few minutes, you gain clarity and process situations more effectively.
📈 Positive Nudges Over Time
"Most of us won't point to the 'one big thing' that changed our organization forever. That's ok." Make small incremental changes that compound. Look for efficiency gains in your daily tasks. This creates a compounding effect that organizations want to keep and promote.
🎉 Celebrate the Inputs, Not Just Outputs
"Can you sleep tonight knowing you maximized the inputs you can control?" Whether passing the CPA exam or achieving tenure—control what you can control. Celebrate that you left it all on the field. When results meet/exceed expectations, it's sweeter. When they don't, you did what you could.
🔁 Yesterday's Win ≠ Today's Victory
"Congrats on the win, time to stop patting self on back and get to figuring out how to achieve today's victory." If you're doing the same job the same way today as five years ago, you've fallen behind.
🔨 Consistently Do the Small Things
"90% of issues can be avoided by just doing the small things all the time." Want happy customers, processes, and results? Do the small stuff...always. Don't stop. Be purposeful with your effort.
💰 How It Affects the Back-Pocket
"People will find the path of least resistance to increase their back-pocket." Understand this when creating incentives. Know ALL the paths that lead to increased back-pockets—and use controls to block the paths you don't want traveled.
⚖️ Know Your Red Line
"There is a near 100 percent probability that YOU will be faced with an ethical dilemma." Define your red line NOW—what you're not willing to do ethically. Because you WILL be asked to cross it. If you do, they have you. They know you'll go further. Say no. Get fired if necessary. "The reputation of a thousand years may be determined by the conduct of one hour." You can always get another job if you're invaluable. You cannot get another reputation.
😊 Be Happy, Be Nice
From "A Gentleman in Moscow": "Unlike adults, children WANT to be happy." If we can't be nice, we're probably far from being happy. Or perhaps if we want happiness, we will be nice. Either way: (1) Be happy, (2) Be nice, (3) Take pleasure in the simplest things.
🌎 Life is About Experiences
"I tell my daughters all the time: life is about experiences." Disney when they were little. International travel when they were older. Madrid—where we went to a bullfight even though they were adamantly opposed to the bull dying. I told them: bullfighting is part of their culture. You need to experience their culture. The non-linear career path? That's the same principle. Each pivot was an uncomfortable experience that paid dividends. Growth lives outside comfort zones.
⏰ The Yesterdays Made Today
Try not to look back questioning yourself. "I wish I would have done that or not done that." Yes, we all make seemingly unwise decisions at times. However, those decisions lead you somewhere. Each destination point alters the journey. People say it's about the journey, not the destination. That's true, but your life is about BOTH—the journey AND the destination points. If you keep striving for better and moving forward, you'll find even those questionable destination points were beneficial. I don't like saying "have no regrets." I prefer: "The yesterdays made today, and I am looking forward to the tomorrows."

Build Skills, Not Resumes Theme 1

Build a skill set that is in high demand AND that other people are scared to develop. Collect capabilities that make you indispensable, not just employable. "I am a nerd. 🚀
Take the Crazy Risks Theme 2
Leaving a controller role for grad school when money goes to near zero? Risky. Leaving tenure-track academia after becoming Director? Riskier. If you want to be a leader, you have to find opportunities to lead. To be out in front. To be in charge. To accept responsibility. Many people will not do this. You have to find those opportunities to be noticed. And here's the hard truth: You have to find opportunities to FAIL. Which means you absolutely cannot be fearful of failing. Suck it up. Move on. Learn from it. Get better. But safe paths are often slow paths. The off-ramp appeared, Shelley supported it, I took it. Trust your gut when opportunity knocks.
⚡
Outwork Everyone
Four certifications. 20+ publications. Getting a MAcc when you already have your CPA just so you're not the one without a graduate degree. Teaching while doing. Serving on committees. There's no shortcut here—you build reputation and capability through relentless output. This is THE lesson: Never be outworked.

But here's why it matters beyond achievement: Being invaluable gives you the freedom to maintain your ethics. When you're asked to cross your red line—and you will be—you can say no because you can always get another job. Your skill set is your freedom.
⚖️
Leadership Isn't Friendship Theme 3 • NEW
Here's something nobody tells you in leadership classes: Being a leader does not necessarily mean you will be friends with those you lead. I've led friends before. It can get dicey when bad news has to be delivered. Do not let emotions drive your reaction when delivering not-so-great news. Do not play favorites with people you like. This may sound harsh, but in my opinion: Emotions have no business in business. Make business decisions void of your emotions and the emotions of others. You want to be someone people respect and follow. Not necessarily someone they hang out with on weekends.
🎲
Say Yes to Opportunities
Adjunct teaching in Industrial Engineering while doing accounting? Guest lecturing on Lean? Every "extra" thing compounds. What seems random today becomes your edge tomorrow. "There are no traffic jams along the extra mile." — Roger Staubach
👑
Take Charge When It Matters
Became Director. Chaired committees. Led initiatives. When the moment allows, step up. Leadership isn't about titles—it's about willingness to own outcomes. "I would rather you make a wrong decision than not make a decision at all." But remember: don't sacrifice being purposeful in action with being fast in execution.
🧭
Your Gut Knows Theme 4
That voice telling you to leave stability for growth? Listen to it. But here's what "trust your gut" really means: You need to think through major decisions. Discuss them with your significant other. Process the different paths. If you've spent time processing, the decision becomes obvious. PhD. Leaving academia. Everything. These were major decisions that weren't easy to make. But they were obvious after moments of reflection. The career that makes sense on paper often makes the least sense for your potential. I've never made a decision I wasn't comfortable with. I knew it was the right decision because I put in the work to know. Trust yourself more than the plan. Both PhD and leaving academia looked illogical. Both were exactly right.

🎓 The Real Lesson

Right now, you're in a leadership class at Auburn University imagining your path. Maybe it's consulting, corporate, entrepreneurship. Here's the truth: it probably won't be any of those.

And that's spectacular.

Because the best careers aren't linear. They're responsive. They pivot. They compound skills in ways you can't predict. A photo sensor specialist becomes a COO. An accounting professor becomes a business operator. The controller who wore jeans becomes the Director in a suit becomes the COO analyzing rental fleet data.

George Petrie wrote the Auburn Creed in 1943. He believed in hard work, education, honesty, courage, and the human touch. Those principles don't tell you WHAT to do—they tell you HOW to be.

So stop trying to predict your 10-year plan. Instead, build a skill set so valuable that when the unexpected opportunity arrives—and it will—you're ready to say yes.

Two Foundations Are Required:

1. You cannot live the non-linear path alone. Find your Shelley. Find someone who believes in you when you're leaving a CFO role for grad school with near-zero income. Find someone who supports the crazy pivots. The non-linear path requires partnership—not just professional networks, but someone who's in it with you for the long haul.

2. You cannot live the non-linear path without ethics. Know your red line. You will be asked to cross it. Say no. Get fired if necessary. "Never be outworked" isn't just about effort—it's about building a skill set so valuable that you have the freedom to maintain your ethics. You can always get another job. You cannot get another reputation.

Five Leadership Truths:

1. Build a skill set that is in demand AND that others are scared to develop.
Other people's fear creates your opportunity to become invaluable.
2. Find opportunities to lead—even when you might fail. Especially when you might fail.
Many people won't do this. You have to. Suck it up. Move on. Learn. Get better.
3. Leadership isn't friendship. Emotions have no business in business.
Make decisions void of your emotions and the emotions of others.
4. Trust your gut means do the reflection work first.
Discuss with your partner. Process the paths. The decision becomes obvious.
5. Be purposeful. Fill in dots.
Every certification. Every conversation. Every decision. Even wanting to out-dress your peers. Everything matters. You may not know if they'll connect. But if you're purposeful, you'll have dots TO connect. They will connect.
