15th Anniversary · 2011 – 2026

The Stump Fire

Official drink of the Stay-at-Home Tailgate.

October 2011. The wives went to the game. They took the kids. A large tree had just been removed. What happened next is the only photograph anyone took, and the only one anyone needed.

“To the stump.”

What’s in the glass

A smoky Old Fashioned by ancestry. A stump fire by design.

Every part of the drink is a part of the picture. The bourbon is the evening. The orange is the Auburn shirt. The peated scotch is the smoke rolling off the stump. The bitters are the char. And the orange peel, flamed over the glass so the oils spark across the surface, is the fire itself.

Peat first, then orange, then warmth. Roughly the order events unfolded.

The recipes

One for the crowd. One for the anniversary.

The Stump Fire

The house shot · batches for a crowd
  • 1 oz bourbon, 100 proof (Wild Turkey 101, Old Forester 100, or Clyde May’s for the Alabama tie)
  • ½ oz Grand Marnier — the orange is the Auburn shirt
  • 2 dashes Angostura bitters — the char
  • ¼ oz Laphroaig 10, floated on top — the smoke off the stump
  • Orange peel, flamed over the glass — the fire

Build

Stir the bourbon, Grand Marnier and bitters over ice for about ten seconds. Strain into a shot glass. Float the Laphroaig over the back of a spoon. Flame the peel across the top and drop it in.

Batch for 20

20 oz bourbon, 10 oz Grand Marnier and 1 oz Angostura in a bottle, kept on ice. Float the scotch and flame the peel per shot. The float needs a steady hand. Assign it early.

Stump Fire Reserve

The 15th-anniversary pour · a 15 for the 15th
  • 1 oz Bowmore 15 — peated Islay, sherry cask, nearly black in the glass
  • ½ oz Grand Marnier
  • 1 dash Angostura bitters
  • Burnt orange peel
  • One small oak or pecan chip — the stump

Build

Set the wood chip on a heatproof plate and light it with a torch. Invert a rocks glass over it until the glass fills with smoke. Flip the glass, and pour the stirred Bowmore, Grand Marnier and bitters into the smoke. Flame the peel over the top.

Why Bowmore

The sherry weight is what makes the Grand Marnier feel like part of the drink instead of something sitting on top of it. And it’s a 15 for the 15th.

The two moves

Both look harder than they are.

The float

Hold a teaspoon upside down just inside the rim, tip touching the glass wall above the surface of the drink. Pour the Laphroaig slowly onto the back of the spoon so it runs down the wall and spreads across the top instead of plunging in. It sits as a thin layer, which is why the first thing you get is peat smoke. Skip the spoon and it still tastes fine; you just lose the smoke-first effect.

The flamed peel

Cut a coin of orange peel about the size of a quarter, skin side out, as little white pith as possible. Hold it an inch or two above the glass with a lit lighter between the peel and the drink. Squeeze sharply so the oils spray through the flame. You get a half-second burst of sparks and a burnt-orange aroma on the drink. Drop the peel in. Fresh oranges only; a dried-out peel won’t spark.
