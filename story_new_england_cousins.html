New England Cousins

The same Hertfordshire gentry stock that produced Barbara's ancestors sent a branch to the founding of Massachusetts — and put the family's cousins at the center of Puritan New England.

Status of this finding: Documented  ·  as a collateral kinship — a cousin line, not a line of direct descent. Named for what it is.

The finding, in brief

The family's deep English roots are usually told as a straight line down to Barbara and the crossing to Charleston. But a family tree branches sideways as much as it runs down, and one of those side branches went the other way across the Atlantic — a generation earlier, and to a different America. Through the maternal Norton line, this family is cousin to Rev. John Norton of Boston, one of the leading ministers of the first Puritan generation in Massachusetts, and — by his brother's marriage — kin to the circle of Governor John Winthrop, at the very center of the Massachusetts Bay founding.

The Norton line, and its cadet branch

The Nortons enter the family through Anne Norton, first wife of Eustace Needham, whose line runs back to the Nortons of Sharpenhoe in Bedfordshire. Like most gentry houses, the Sharpenhoe Nortons threw off younger, or "cadet," branches — and one of those branches, seated around Bishop's Stortford on the Hertfordshire border, produced two brothers who sailed for New England.

Rev. John Norton of Boston

The elder was John Norton (1606–1663), born at Bishop's Stortford, who crossed to Massachusetts and became teacher of the First Church of Boston — one of the most influential Puritan divines of the colony's founding generation, a writer and controversialist who was later sent back to England on the colony's business. His brother, William Norton, settled at Ipswich in Massachusetts. They belong to the family's extended kin-network as cousins out of the Sharpenhoe cadet branch.

The Winthrop tie

And it reaches one degree further. William Norton of Ipswich married Lucy Downing, a niece of Governor John Winthrop — the Winthrop who led the great migration and served as the first and long-serving governor of Massachusetts Bay. Through that marriage the Winthrop and Downing families — as central to the founding of New England as any — enter the family's wider web of kin.

the Nortons of Sharpenhoe → cadet branch (Bishop's Stortford) → Rev. John Norton of Boston & William Norton of Ipswich → William m. Lucy Downing, niece of Gov. John Winthrop
The same family that sat in Tudor Hertfordshire had cousins helping to build Puritan Boston a generation later.

What this is — and is not

This is a cousinship, not a descent. The direct line runs, as ever, through Anne Norton and down to Barbara; the New England Nortons are a collateral branch off the same Sharpenhoe stock — kin, not ancestors. Held at that honest level, it is still a striking thing: the family's own cousins among the ministers and governors of the founding of New England, documented in the standard genealogies of the Sharpenhoe Nortons and in the early records of Massachusetts.
