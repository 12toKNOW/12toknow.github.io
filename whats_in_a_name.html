A classic English-Southern naming pool — a handful of names, reused every generation. (Count Marys hiding in middle names, like Mary Polly and Mary Baker, and there are even more.)

How the names traveled

The fun part isn't that names repeat — it's why. These are the ones the record actually explains.

Documented

Mary, for a grandmother

Mary Baker DeGraffenreid was "christened Mary Baker for the grandmother she never knew" — Mary Baker of Chowan, the Carolina colonel's daughter. The grandmother's own surname had already become a boy's first name a generation earlier: Baker DeGraffenreid, born 1744.

Documented

Freeman Jones Searcy

Freeman's middle name isn't decoration — it's his mother's family. Aaron Searcy married Martha "Patsy" Jones in 1819, and their son carried the Jones name forward, the maternal line folded into a first-and-middle.

The through-line

Barbara & Christopher

The Englishwoman who crossed the Atlantic in 1714 and the man who waited for her on the Charleston dock. Both names echo down the centuries — five Barbaras in the record, right down to a great-aunt the family still calls Aunt Bob.

So popular it caused trouble

The Aarons

The name was reused so faithfully that untangling which Aaron fathered Freeman took a Revolutionary War pension file to settle. That's a story of its own →

The surname habit

The family's favorite way to honor a line was to carry its surname forward as a first or middle name — so a maiden name never quite disappears. You can watch it happen right in the register:
