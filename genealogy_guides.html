A 12toKNOW Guide

The Genealogy Guides

Two method-driven kits for doing family history the disciplined way — using AI to reach further, without ever letting it invent what isn't there. Built from real work on a real family.

Free to download & use

Most "AI genealogy" advice is either breathless hype or a warning to stay away. These two kits take a third path — the practitioner's path. They show exactly how to put a capable model to work on the parts it's genuinely good at (organizing, cross-referencing, drafting), while keeping the human firmly in charge of the one thing that matters most: whether a claim is actually true.

The through-line: the machine can reach further and faster, but a fact isn't a fact until a human has tied it to a source a stranger could check. Every method in these kits is built to keep that line bright.

The Complete Kit · Research

Researching a Family History with Claude

A three-part guide · ~4,500 words · 12 pages

Turning curiosity into proof a stranger can trust — the evidence discipline that keeps AI-assisted research honest.

  • The Guide — the five evidence tiers, the Ahnentafel numbering, the FAN / neighbor-cluster method, DNA as its own witness, and society-standard proof.
  • The Toolkit — 11 ready-to-use templates and checklists (standing instructions, citation template, DNA intake, application audit, session hand-off, and more).
  • An Annotated Sample — one real problem worked end to end, showing the whole method in a single case.

The complete ten-guide Genealogy Methodology is the reference underneath them — evidence grading, pedigree numbering, the DNA workflow, source-documentation standards, lineage-society applications, FAN-cluster reconstruction, and more, each generalized so you can adopt it on your own family.

How to use these

They're free — take them, use them on your own family, adapt the templates, share them with anyone who'd find them useful. The research kit comes first; the writing kit assumes you've done the proof work it describes. A credit back to 12toKNOW is appreciated but never required.
